How the Ranking Works

A plain-language explanation of the Elo system used in this app.

The Big Idea

Every player starts with a rating of 1500. After each match, the winner gains points and the loser loses the same amount.

The key insight: beating a stronger player earns more points than beating a weaker one. If you were already expected to win, you gain little. If you pull off an upset, you gain a lot.

This means rankings reflect quality of wins, not just number of wins.

Step 1 — Expected Score

Before each match, the system calculates how likely each player is to win, based on the rating gap:

E = 1 / (1 + 10((R_opp − R_you) / 400))

This gives a number between 0 and 1 — your expected score.

  • Same rating → E ≈ 0.50 (50% chance each)
  • +200 rating advantage → E ≈ 0.76
  • +400 rating advantage → E ≈ 0.91
Step 2 — Rating Update

After the match, each player's rating is updated:

new_rating = old_rating + K_effective × (actual − expected)

The actual score is:

  • Win → 1.0
  • Draw → 0.5
  • Loss → 0.0

If your expected score was 0.70 and you won (1.0), the difference is +0.30 → you gain points. If you lost (0.0), the difference is −0.70 → you lose quite a lot, because that was an upset.

K Factor — Speed of Change

The K factor controls how many points can be gained or lost in a single match. We use a provisional K that starts high and decreases as a player builds a match history:

24

20
Games 16+

When two players have different K values, the match uses the average of the two.

New players' ratings move fast at first so they reach their true level quickly. After 15 games, ratings are considered reliable and change more slowly.

Same-Day Rematch Damping

If two players play multiple times on the same day, later rematches carry less weight. This prevents someone from farming rating points through endless sessions against the same opponent:

The first two matches of the day between the same pair are fully weighted. A third is worth 75%, a fourth and beyond 50%.

Putting It All Together

The final effective K for any match is:

K_effective = base_K × goal_diff_multiplier × repeat_multiplier

And the rating update is:

new_rating = old_rating + K_effective × (actual − expected)

Ratings are always recomputed from scratch in chronological order whenever any match is added, edited or deleted. This ensures historical consistency.

Worked Example

Alice (rating 1600, 20 games) beats Bob (rating 1400, 3 games) 3–1. It's their first match today.

1

Expected scores

Alice: E = 1/(1+10((1400−1600)/400)) = 1/(1+10−0.5) ≈ 0.76

Bob: E ≈ 0.24

2

K factor

Alice (20 games) → K = 20. Bob (3 games) → K = 40. Average = 30

3

Multipliers

Goal diff = 2 → ×1.05. First match today → ×1.00. K_eff = 30 × 1.05 × 1.00 = 31.5

4

Rating change

Alice: +31.5 × (1.0 − 0.76) = +7.6 → 1607.6

Bob: +31.5 × (0.0 − 0.24) = −7.6 → 1392.4

Alice gains only 7.6 points despite winning comfortably — she was heavily favoured. If Bob had pulled the upset, he would have gained around 23.7 points.
